The Price of Getting Sick in Abuja

What happens when healthcare costs more than a family can bear.

It is early morning in Abuja. A mother sits outside a primary healthcare centre with her child beside her. She has already spent money on transport. She is now waiting to find out how much the treatment will cost.

For many families, getting sick is not only a health concern. It is a financial one.

Healthcare is often described as a basic need, but access to it can depend heavily on how much a person can afford at the moment they need it.

A consultation, laboratory test, medication or hospital admission may each seem like a manageable expense on their own. But when several costs come together, the total can quickly become too much for a household to carry.

The financial burden does not always begin inside the hospital. It can start before a person even reaches a health facility.

Transport costs, consultation fees, diagnostic tests, prescribed medicines and follow-up visits can all add to the final amount. For someone living on a limited income, these expenses can compete directly with food, rent, school fees and other household needs.

This is where the idea of affordability becomes important. Healthcare may technically be available, but availability does not always mean that everyone can comfortably access it.

“The question is not only whether healthcare exists, but whether people can afford to use it when they need it.”

When people cannot afford care

Sometimes the cost changes the decision itself.

When healthcare becomes difficult to afford, people may delay seeking treatment, purchase only some of the medicines prescribed, choose a cheaper facility or rely on advice from friends and family.

These decisions are not necessarily about people not caring about their health. Sometimes they are practical responses to limited resources.

What can change?

Making healthcare easier to reach should go beyond building more facilities.

Improving access also means thinking about what happens between the moment someone becomes sick and the moment they receive the care they need.

Strengthening affordable primary healthcare services.

Reducing unnecessary financial barriers to essential care.

03

Expanding awareness of available health services and support.

04

Improving health insurance coverage and understanding.

05

Making preventive healthcare more accessible before illness becomes expensive to manage.

Getting sick should not automatically mean choosing between healthcare and other basic needs.

Abuja is a growing city with expanding healthcare services, but access is about more than having hospitals and clinics around. It is also about whether people can reach them, afford them and receive the care they need without being pushed into financial difficulty.

Because sometimes, the price of getting sick is more than the hospital bill.
